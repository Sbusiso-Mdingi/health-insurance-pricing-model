# Health Insurance Pricing Methodology

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document sets out the method behind a frequency-severity premium for a generic health
insurance product on a synthetic South African portfolio: how the portfolio is generated, how
the models are chosen and fitted, how the premium, its interval and the tariff are calculated,
and how the results are checked.

All data is **fully synthetic**, generated by `scripts/simulate.R` from illustrative parameters.
The outputs are not real rates. Because the true model is known, every fitted effect and every
interval can be checked against it, which is not possible with real data.

---

## 2. Pricing Framework

**Expected claims cost = Expected claims per policy year × Expected cost per claim**

**Tariff premium = Expected claims cost × tariff(age) / factor(age, sex)** (section 7)

**Annual premium = Tariff premium × (1 + loading)**, and the monthly premium is one twelfth
of that. The calculator's default loading of 15% for expenses and margin is an assumption, not
a fitted quantity.

Frequency and severity are modelled separately so that each factor's effect on how often a
member claims and on how much each claim costs can be seen on its own. Plans are the clearest
case: out-of-hospital cover means many more claims, each much smaller.

---

## 3. Synthetic Portfolio

One portfolio of 100,000 principal members (seed 123), each with:

- **Age** 18 to 85, drawn as 18 + 67 × Beta(2, 2.8), so the book is working-age heavy.
- **Sex**, 52% female.
- **Province**, one of the nine, weighted to where private cover is concentrated: Gauteng 38%,
  Western Cape 17%, KwaZulu-Natal 15%, Eastern Cape 7%, Mpumalanga 6%, Free State, Limpopo and
  North West 5% each, Northern Cape 2%.
- **Children**, Poisson with a mean peaking at 1.8 for members aged 40, capped at 5.
- **Smoker**, logistic in sex and age: 12.5% of women and 31% of men.
- **BMI**, lognormal around 26, higher for women and rising with age to 60, clipped to 16 to 50.
- **Plan**, one of three benefit tiers, from a multinomial logit: members with children lean to
  Standard, older members to Comprehensive. Essential covers in-hospital treatment; Standard
  adds out-of-hospital specialists, scans and tests; Comprehensive adds GP visits, prescribed
  medicines, dental and optical.
- **Exposure**, a full year for 80% of policies and uniform between 0.1 and 1 otherwise.

**Claim counts** are Poisson given a gamma frailty with mean 1 and variance 0.3, so they are
negative binomial with theta = 3.33. On the log scale the rate rises 0.025 a year with age, with
an extra 0.0006 per squared year above 55, a maternity peak of 0.45 for women at age 31, 0.035
per BMI point above 25 and 0.05 per point below 20, 0.26 for smokers and 0.22 per child. Standard
and Comprehensive multiply the rate by 4 and 10. Provinces outside Gauteng are 3% to 15% lower.

**Claim costs** are drawn claim by claim: a scale from the member's profile times a lognormal
(sdlog 1.1) with probability 0.97 or a Pareto large claim (alpha 2.3) with probability 0.03. The
expected cost is R45,000 a claim for the base profile, rising 0.8% a year of age and 1.5% per BMI
point above 25, 22% higher for smokers, 5% higher for men and 7% lower per child. Standard and
Comprehensive claims are 0.45 and 0.25 times the size of Essential claims, and provinces outside
Gauteng are up to 10% cheaper, except Western Cape at 2% dearer. The squared coefficient of
variation of a single claim, the Gamma model's true dispersion, is 3.37.

The resulting portfolio averages 2.20 claims per policy year at R21,002 a claim. The plan mix is
34.7% Essential, 47.7% Standard and 17.6% Comprehensive.

## 4. Models

The portfolio is split at random into 80% training and 20% holdout (seed 2026). Candidates, all
with Smoker, Children, Province and Plan:

| Name | Age, BMI and sex terms |
| --- | --- |
| F1, S1 | linear Age and BMI, Sex |
| F2, S2 | natural splines ns(Age, 4) and ns(BMI, 4), Sex |
| F3 | ns(Age, 10) × Sex interaction, ns(BMI, 4) |
| S3 | ns(Age, 4) × Sex interaction, ns(BMI, 4) |

**Frequency** is a GLM with a log link and log exposure as an offset, fitted both as Poisson
and as negative binomial (`MASS::glm.nb`). The Poisson fit has a Pearson dispersion of 1.60,
well above 1, and the negative binomial estimates theta = 3.32 (standard error 0.05) against the
true 3.33, so the negative binomial is used.

**Severity** is a Gamma GLM with a log link on the average cost per claim of policies with
claims, weighted by the number of claims. Since the variance of an average of k claims is the
per-claim variance over k, this is equivalent to fitting every claim. The estimated dispersion
is 3.05 against a true 3.37; the Pareto tail makes the Pearson estimate noisy.

**Selection** uses nested tests at the 5% level: likelihood ratio tests for frequency, and F
tests with the estimated dispersion for severity, where the Gamma AIC is unreliable with heavy
tails.

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| F1 to F2 | LR 713.3, p < 0.001 |
| F2 to F3 | p < 0.001 |
| S1 to S2 | F 2.19, p = 0.041 |
| S2 to S3 | F 1.36, p = 0.243 |

The final models are F3 and S2. The rejection of S3 is correct: the true severity has no age by
sex interaction. F3's age spline has 10 degrees of freedom, chosen by AIC among 6, 8, 10 and 12
(10 gives 249,524.4 against 249,532.0 for 6).

**Single Tweedie GLM.** For comparison, a Tweedie GLM (power 1.6, log link) with F3's terms is
fitted directly to the cost per policy year, weighted by exposure.

## 5. Premium Interval

On the log scale, the expected claims cost is x_f'b_f + x_s'b_s, the sum of the two models'
linear predictors. The frequency and severity likelihoods factorise (counts and costs share no
parameters), so the two estimates are independent and

**Var = x_f' V_f x_f + x_s' V_s x_s**

where V_f and V_s are the coefficient covariance matrices. The 95% interval is
exp(log premium ± 1.96 × standard error). It reflects estimation error only: it is not a range
for one member's claims, and it does not cover a wrong model.

## 6. Validation

On the 20,225 holdout policies (18,451 policy years, 40,140 claims):

| Model | Tweedie deviance explained | Gini | Actual over expected cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 × S1, linear | 15.4% | 0.363 | 0.991 |
| F2 × S2, splines | 15.8% | 0.367 | 0.987 |
| F3 × S2, final | 15.9% | 0.369 | 0.988 |
| F3 × S3 | 15.9% | 0.369 | 0.988 |
| Tweedie GLM, F3 terms | 15.9% | 0.369 | 0.987 |
| True model | 15.9% | 0.369 | 0.984 |

The final pair explains 50.5% of frequency deviance and 26.7% of severity deviance, and the
actual over expected claim count is 0.999. Cost is 1.2% below expected, but so is the true
model's, so this is large-claim noise rather than bias. By plan, actual over expected cost is
0.964 for Essential, 0.979 for Standard and 1.018 for Comprehensive. Of the 25 relativity intervals,
24 contain the true value. `reports/validation.md` has the full tables, including by province,
and the lift by decile.

**Coverage study.** `scripts/coverage_study.R` simulates 200 portfolios of 80,000 policies,
refits the final models and the Poisson alternative on each, and counts how often the 95%
intervals contain the true value (Monte Carlo standard error about 1.5 points):

| Intervals | Coverage | Mean SE ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency, negative binomial | 95.0% | 0.99 |
| Frequency, Poisson | 83.3% | 0.70 |
| Severity, Gamma | 94.7% | 1.00 |
| Premium, negative binomial × Gamma | 92.5% | 0.99 |
| Premium, Poisson × Gamma | 87.5% | 0.84 |

The Poisson standard errors are about 30% too small. Premium coverage is 88.0% for a woman of 40
and 91.5% for a woman of 31, and 93.0% to 94.5% for the other four profiles, because the age
spline still smooths part of the maternity peak (log bias 0.019 at 40). The first run, with 6
degrees of freedom, covered these two profiles only 77% to 80% of the time, which is why the
degrees of freedom are now chosen by AIC. `reports/coverage.md` has every row.

## 7. Tariff and Cross-Subsidy

The fitted age and sex factor for women rises to the maternity peak at about 31, dips and rises
again. A rate table in which the premium falls as a member ages would be hard to justify, and
charging the maternity cost only to women of childbearing age would make cover dearest for them
when they are most likely to need it. The tariff therefore charges women and men one curve in
age:

1. **Unisex factor.** At each age, u(age) = Σ_s w_s(age) f_s(age) / Σ_s w_s(age), where f_s is
   the fitted age and sex factor for sex s and

   **w_s(age) = Σ over members of that age and sex of exposure × risk premium / f_s(age)**,

   the premium each age and sex would carry with a factor of 1.
2. **Graduation.** log g minimises Σ c(age) (log g − log u)² + λ Σ (Δ² log g)², the
   Whittaker-Henderson criterion, with c(age) each age's expected claims over the average age's
   and λ = 100. λ = 18 is the least smoothing that makes the curve non-decreasing, but it leaves
   a near-flat stretch from 34 to 38; at 100 the curve rises at least 1.1% a year at every age.
3. **Rescale.** g is multiplied by a constant so that Σ (w_female + w_male) g = Σ_s Σ w_s f_s:
   the tariff collects the same total premium as the risk premium on the training portfolio.

Every member's premium is multiplied by g(age) / f_s(age). On the training portfolio, the tariff
over the risk premium is:

| Age band | Women | Men |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18 to 24 | 1.037 | 1.053 |
| 25 to 29 | 0.904 | 1.153 |
| 30 to 34 | 0.819 | 1.151 |
| 35 to 39 | 0.965 | 1.093 |
| 40 to 44 | 1.009 | 1.006 |
| 45 to 49 | 0.997 | 0.993 |
| 50 to 59 | 1.022 | 0.975 |
| 60 to 69 | 1.030 | 0.970 |
| 70 to 85 | 1.032 | 0.964 |
| All ages | 0.996 | 1.005 |

Men aged 25 to 39 carry most of the maternity cost. Above 50 the flow reverses, because older
men claim more than older women, so the net subsidy between the sexes is under 1% of premium.
Members aged 18 to 24 pay a little more than their expected claims because the smoothing lifts
the steep start of the curve, where there are few members. A unisex tariff collects the right
total only for the sex and age mix it was priced on.

---

## 8. Risk Classes

Members are placed in quartiles of expected claims cost among holdout members on the same plan.
Comparing across plans would mostly measure the benefits bought, not the member's risk.

---

## 9. Limitations

- Synthetic data with illustrative parameters, and a generic product not designed against any
  particular regulatory framework.
- Principal members only; no adult dependants, waiting periods, exclusions, excesses, benefit
  limits or co-payments. The tiers differ only in scope of cover.
- The tariff only smooths the age curve; it does not round to age bands or cap year-on-year
  increases, and as a unisex rate it is exposed to a change in the sex mix.
- Frequency and severity are independent by construction.
- A flat loading, and no inflation, trend or credibility weighting.
